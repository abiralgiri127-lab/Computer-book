Unit 8: Information Security and Cyber Law


8.1 Introduction to Information Security

Definition: Information Security (InfoSec) refers to the processes, tools, and policies designed to protect digital and physical information from unauthorized access, use, disclosure, disruption, modification, or destruction.

The CIA Triad: The foundational security framework consisting of three core principles:

Confidentiality: Ensuring that sensitive data is accessible only to authorized users and protected from unauthorized viewing.

Integrity: Maintaining the accuracy, completeness, and reliability of data by protecting it from unauthorized modification or tampering.

Availability: Ensuring that information systems and data are operational and accessible to authorized users whenever needed.


8.2 Security Threats and Vulnerabilities

Security Threat: Any potential event, action, or circumstance that can cause harm, loss, or unauthorized access to computer systems and data.

Vulnerability: A weakness or flaw in system hardware, software, network design, or operational procedures that can be exploited by a threat.


8.2.1 Malicious Software (Malware)

Virus: A self-replicating program that attaches itself to clean executable files and spreads from system to system when the host program is executed.

Worm: A standalone malicious program that replicates itself across computer networks automatically without requiring human interaction or a host file.

Trojan Horse: Malicious software disguised as legitimate, safe software to trick users into installing it, creating a backdoor for unauthorized access.

Ransomware: Malware that encrypts the victim's files or locks system access, demanding a ransom payment to restore access.

Spyware: Software that covertly monitors user activity, logs keystrokes, and gathers personal information without consent.

Adware: Software that automatically displays or downloads unwanted advertisements, often bundled with free software.


8.2.2 Cyber Attacks and Network Threats

Phishing: Social engineering attacks using fraudulent emails or messages designed to trick users into revealing sensitive credentials or personal data.

Denial of Service (DoS) / Distributed DoS (DDoS): An attack aimed at overwhelming a network server or service with excessive traffic, rendering it unavailable to legitimate users.

Man-in-the-Middle (MitM) Attack: An attack where the perpetrator secretly intercepts and potentially alters communication between two relaying parties.

Spoofing: Falsifying network addresses or sender identity (e.g., IP spoofing, email spoofing) to disguise oneself as a trusted source.

Unauthorized Access / Hacking: Gaining entry into a computer system or network without permission.

Ethical Hacker (White Hat): Identifies system vulnerabilities with permission to improve security.

Cracker (Black Hat): Breaks into systems with malicious intent for data theft or disruption.


8.3 Security Measures and Protection

Authentication: Verifying the identity of a user or system (e.g., Passwords, Biometrics, Multi-Factor Authentication/MFA).

Authorization: Granting specific access rights and permissions to an authenticated user.

Cryptography / Encryption:

Encryption: Transforming readable plain text into unreadable ciphertext using an algorithm and encryption key.

Decryption: Reversing the process to restore ciphertext back into readable plain text.

Firewall: Hardware or software network security systems that monitor and filter incoming and outgoing network traffic based on predefined security rules.

Antivirus Software: Programs designed to scan, detect, quarantine, and remove malware from a storage device.

Digital Signature: A mathematical technique used to validate the authenticity, integrity, and non-repudiation of a digital document or message.

Backup & Data Recovery: Regularly copying data to secondary storage media (cloud or external drives) to enable restoration in the event of system failure or ransomware attack.


8.4 Cyber Ethics and Cyber Law


8.4.1 Cyber Ethics

Definition: The moral principles and behavioral standards governing the responsible use of computers, networks, and digital resources.

Key Ethical Practices: Respecting copyright and intellectual property rights, avoiding digital piracy, respecting personal privacy, and refraining from cyberbullying or harassment.


8.4.2 Cyber Law and Intellectual Property Rights

Cyber Law: The legal framework dealing with internet usage, digital communication, software protection, online transactions, and cybercrime prosecution.

Cybercrime: Any illegal activity conducted through or against computer systems, networks, or digital devices.

Intellectual Property Rights (IPR): Legal protections granted to creators of original digital or physical works.

Copyright: Legal right protecting original written content, software source code, music, and art from unauthorized copying.

Patent: Legal protection granted for unique technical inventions, algorithms, or hardware designs.

Trademark: Protects unique brand symbols, logos, names, and slogans.


8.4.3 Cyber Law in Nepal (The Electronic Transaction Act, 2063)

ETA 2063: Promulgated in Nepal in 2063 B.S. (2006 A.D.) to provide legal recognition to electronic transactions, digital signatures, and enforce penal provisions against cybercrimes.

Key Objectives:

Legalize and regulate electronic records and digital signatures.

Control illegal activities, unauthorized access, and hacking targeting digital communications.

Establish specialized IT tribunals to resolve disputes and prosecute cyber offenses.
