Unit 1: Computer System


1.1 Introduction to Computer


1.1.1 Definition, Characteristics, and Applications

Definition:
A computer is an electronic data-processing machine that accepts raw data as input, processes it sequentially according to stored instructions, produces meaningful information as output, and saves the results for future use.

Data Processing Cycle:
Input (Data) -> Processing (CPU) -> Output (Information) -> Storage (Memory)


Key Characteristics:


Speed: Operates at electronic speeds, executing millions to billions of operations per second (measured in MHz or GHz).

Accuracy: Delivers error-free results, provided the input data and commands are correct (Principle of GIGO: Garbage In, Garbage Out).

Diligence: Operates continuously without human fatigue, boredom, or loss of concentration.

Versatility: Performs diverse tasks, ranging from basic numerical arithmetic to complex scientific modeling.

Storage: Holds vast amounts of data permanently in secondary storage devices for later retrieval.

Automation: Executes complex sequences of instructions automatically without manual intervention once started.


Major Applications:


Education: E-learning platforms, administrative record management, and computer-based research.

Banking and Finance: Core banking operations, online transaction processing, and automated ledger balance calculations.

Medicine and Health: Diagnostic imaging (CT scans, MRI), patient records management, and real-time vital tracking.

Government: E-governance portals, census operations, tax collection systems, and national identity maintenance.

Science and Engineering: Weather forecasting, structural simulations, satellite tracking, and CAD (Computer-Aided Design).


1.1.2 Evolution of Computer Technology

1st Generation (1940–1956):

Core Technology: Vacuum Tubes

Primary Memory: Magnetic Drums

Secondary Storage: Magnetic Tapes, Punch Cards

Programming Language: Machine Language (Binary)

Characteristics: Huge physical size, massive heat production, low reliability.

Examples: ENIAC, UNIVAC-I, EDVAC.

2nd Generation (1956–1963):

Core Technology: Transistors

Primary Memory: Magnetic Core Memory

Secondary Storage: Magnetic Disks

Programming Language: Assembly Language, Early High-Level (FORTRAN, COBOL)

Characteristics: Smaller, faster, and more energy-efficient than vacuum tube systems.

Examples: IBM 1401, CDC 1604.

3rd Generation (1964–1971):

Core Technology: Integrated Circuits (ICs)

Primary Memory: Semiconductor RAM / ROM

Secondary Storage: Magnetic Disks

Programming Language: High-Level Languages (BASIC, Pascal, C)

Characteristics: Keyboard/monitor interaction replacing punch cards; multi-programming OS.

Examples: IBM 360 series, PDP-8.

4th Generation (1971–Present):

Core Technology: VLSI & ULSI Microprocessors

Primary Memory: Semiconductor Memory

Secondary Storage: Hard Disks, SSDs, Optical Storage

Programming Language: Modern High-Level (C++, Java, Python)

Characteristics: Desktop personal computers, high speed, portable, integrated circuits with millions of transistors.

Examples: Intel Core series, Apple M series.

5th Generation (Present & Future):

Core Technology: AI, Parallel Processing & Quantum Logic

Primary Memory: High-Density Semiconductors

Secondary Storage: Cloud & Ultra-Density Storage

Programming Language: Natural Languages, AI Frameworks

Characteristics: Natural language recognition, automated decision-making, expert systems, parallel processing architectures.


1.1.3 Units of Measurement for Speed and Storage

Processor Speed Measurement:

Clock Speed (Hz): Represents the number of pulses generated by the system clock per second.

1 Hertz (Hz) = 1 cycle per second

1 Megahertz (MHz) = 1,000,000 cycles per second

1 Gigahertz (GHz) = 1,000,000,000 cycles per second

MIPS: Millions of Instructions Per Second.

FLOPS: Floating-Point Operations Per Second (used for measuring supercomputer performance).

Data Storage Units:

Bit: The smallest data unit (represents binary 0 or 1).

Nibble: 4 Bits

Byte: 8 Bits (stores a single ASCII character)

Kilobyte (KB): 1,024 Bytes

Megabyte (MB): 1,024 KB

Gigabyte (GB): 1,024 MB

Terabyte (TB): 1,024 GB

Petabyte (PB): 1,024 TB


1.1.4 Classification of Computers


1. On the Basis of Work / Operational Principle:


Analog Computers: Measure continuous physical quantities (e.g., voltage, temperature, pressure). Examples: Speedometers, analog thermometers.

Digital Computers: Process discrete numerical values represented in binary digits (0 and 1). Examples: Personal computers, smartphones.

Hybrid Computers: Combine features of both analog (fast signal processing) and digital (high precision storage/calculation) machines. Examples: ECG devices in hospitals, petrol pump dispensers.


2. On the Basis of Size and Capacity:


Supercomputers: Fastest, largest, and most expensive computers designed for complex scientific problems. Used for weather forecasting, molecular modeling, and nuclear research. Examples: Frontier, Fugaku, PARAM.

Mainframe Computers: Large-scale systems capable of managing thousands of simultaneous user connections and accessing high volumes of central storage. Used in credit card processing, banking backends, and airline reservations. Examples: IBM zSeries.

Minicomputers: Mid-sized multi-user systems offering performance intermediate between microcomputers and mainframes. Used in small-to-medium enterprise data management. Examples: DEC VAX, AS/400.

Microcomputers: Personal computing systems powered by a single microprocessor IC. Includes desktop PCs, laptops, and tablets.


1.1.5 Mobile Computing and Applications

Definition:
Mobile Computing refers to a framework that enables the transmission of data, voice, and video via a computer or wireless device without fixed physical connections.

Three Core Components:

Mobile Hardware: Compact portable hardware devices (Smartphones, Tablets, Wearables) equipped with sensors and cellular/Wi-Fi chips.

Mobile Software: Specialized operating systems (Android, iOS) and mobile apps optimized for low power consumption and touch control interfaces.

Mobile Communication: Wireless infrastructure (GSM, 4G/5G, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth) facilitating seamless network packet delivery on the move.


Key Applications:


Mobile banking and digital wallets (e.g., eSewa, Khalti, direct mobile banking).

Location-Based Services (LBS) and real-time GPS navigation systems.

Remote work, cloud storage integration, and mobile productivity suites.

M-Commerce (online shopping via mobile platforms).


1.2 Computer System Architecture and Internal Components


1.2.1 Concepts of Computer Architecture and Organization

Computer Architecture: Refers to the conceptual design and fundamental operational structure of a computer system as seen by a software programmer. It defines the instruction set, data types, addressing modes, and register structures.

Computer Organization: Deals with the operational hardware units and their interconnections that realize the architectural specifications (e.g., clock frequencies, memory technologies, physical circuit interfaces).

Von Neumann Architecture Model: A fundamental design model specifying that CPU execution relies on a central processing unit, unified memory holding both program instructions and data, and sequential execution of instructions using an instruction cycle (Fetch-Decode-Execute).


1.2.2 Five Core Functional Components

Input Unit: Converts user data and commands from human-readable format into binary machine code suitable for processing.

Processing Unit (CPU): Executes instructions, performs calculations, and manages hardware subsystems.

Output Unit: Translates processed binary data back into human-understandable visual, textual, or auditory forms.

Memory Unit (Primary Storage): Provides short-term storage for active programs, execution context, and immediate data required by the CPU.

Storage Unit (Secondary Storage): Provides permanent, non-volatile capacity to save system software, user files, and application code.


1.2.3 Microprocessor Fundamentals

Definition:
A Microprocessor is an integrated circuit (IC) that contains the complete Central Processing Unit (CPU) on a single silicon chip.

Key Technical Metrics:

Clock Speed: The rate at which the processor executes basic instruction steps, measured in Gigahertz (GHz).

Word Length: The total number of data bits a CPU can process in a single clock cycle (e.g., 32-bit or 64-bit architecture).

Internal Components:

Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU): Performs mathematical calculations (addition, subtraction, multiplication) and logical decisions (AND, OR, NOT, comparison).

Control Unit (CU): Directs signal flow within the CPU, fetching instructions from main memory, decoding them, and issuing control signals to other units.

Registers: High-speed temporary storage cells located directly inside the CPU core.

Program Counter (PC): Holds the memory address of the next instruction to be fetched.

Instruction Register (IR): Holds the instruction currently being decoded/executed.

Accumulator (ACC): Stores intermediate calculation results from the ALU.

Memory Address Register (MAR): Holds the memory address being accessed for read/write operations.


1.2.4 Bus System

Definition:
A bus is a collection of parallel physical wires or circuit traces used to transmit signals between system hardware components.

Types of System Buses:

Data Bus: Carries actual raw data and instructions between CPU, memory, and peripheral controllers. It is Bi-directional.

Address Bus: Transmits physical memory addresses specifying where data should be read from or written to. It is Uni-directional (CPU to devices).

Control Bus: Carries command, timing, and status signals (such as Memory Read, Memory Write, I/O Read, Interrupt Request). It is Bi-directional / Multi-line.


1.2.5 Primary Memory

Primary memory directly interacts with the processor to hold currently executing programs and temporary data.

A. RAM (Random Access Memory):

Characteristics: Volatile storage (contents clear when system power is turned off); read/write access capability.

SRAM (Static RAM): Built using transistor flip-flops; extremely fast; does not require periodic refresh cycles; used for CPU Cache.

DRAM (Dynamic RAM): Built using paired transistor-capacitor storage cells; requires continuous electrical refresh cycles; used for main system memory (e.g., DDR4, DDR5).

B. ROM (Read-Only Memory):

Characteristics: Non-volatile storage (retains data permanently without power supply); contains factory-written initialization microcode (BIOS/UEFI).

PROM (Programmable ROM): Blank chip that can be programmed once using a specialized device.

EPROM (Erasable Programmable ROM): Erased by exposing the chip to high-intensity Ultraviolet (UV) light.

EEPROM (Electrically Erasable Programmable ROM): Erased and rewritten electrically byte-by-byte (used in modern flash BIOS).

C. Cache Memory and Buffer:

Cache Memory: Small, ultra-fast semiconductor memory placed directly on or adjacent to the CPU core. It holds frequently accessed instructions to reduce CPU wait time relative to main RAM speeds.

Buffer: A temporary memory reservation space used to match data transfer speeds between devices operating at different performance rates.


1.2.6 Secondary Memory

Secondary memory provides high-capacity, non-volatile, long-term storage for system data and user media.

Magnetic Disks (e.g., Hard Disk Drive - HDD): Data is written by magnetizing microscopic surface sectors on rotating circular platters.

Flash Memory (e.g., Solid State Drives - SSD, USB Flash Drives): Non-volatile semiconductor storage with no moving mechanical parts. Offers higher read/write speeds, lower latency, and better shock resistance compared to HDDs.

Optical Disks: Data is read using laser beams focused on reflective polycarbonate tracks with micro-pits and lands (CD-ROM ~700 MB, DVD-ROM ~4.7 GB to 8.5 GB, Blu-Ray ~25 GB to 50 GB).

External Storage: Portable secondary storage media (e.g., external HDDs, external SSDs, SD cards) connected via external hardware ports.


1.2.7 Input Devices

Keyboard: Primary text and command input device using physical key switches.

Mouse / Touchpad: Pointing devices that convert physical 2D motion coordinates into cursor movements.

Scanner: Converts physical documents and images into digital raster graphics files.

OMR (Optical Mark Reader): Detects shaded pencil or ink marks on standardized forms.

OCR (Optical Character Recognition): Converts scanned images of printed/handwritten text into editable digital text files.

BCR (Barcode Reader): Emits light beams to read printed parallel line spacing patterns into numerical data.

MICR (Magnetic Ink Character Reader): Reads characters printed with magnetic iron-oxide ink (used in bank checks).

Touchscreen: Dual input/output display surface detecting finger touch placement via capacitive or resistive grids.

Microphone: Converts acoustic sound waves into digital audio signals.


1.2.8 Output Devices

Monitors (Soft-Copy Output):

LCD (Liquid Crystal Display): Uses liquid crystals sandwiched between polarizing filters, illuminated by CCFL backlight.

LED (Light Emitting Diode): Uses energy-efficient LED arrays to backlight an LCD panel.

Printers (Hard-Copy Output):

Impact Printers: Physical mechanical contact strikes an inked ribbon onto paper (e.g., Dot Matrix Printer).

Non-Impact Printers: No physical striking mechanism touches the paper surface (e.g., Inkjet Printer using liquid ink nozzles; Laser Printer using laser beams, static drum, and dry powdered toner).

Speakers / Headphones: Convert digital audio signals back into audible sound waves.


1.2.9 Hardware Interfaces and Expansion Slots

Serial Port (COM Port / RS-232): Transfers data sequentially one bit at a time over a single communication line.

Parallel Port (LPT Port): Transfers multiple data bits (typically 8 bits) simultaneously over parallel wires.

USB (Universal Serial Bus) Ports: High-speed, hot-swappable, universal standard interfaces supplying both data transfer lines and peripheral power (Type-A, Type-B, Type-C).

HDMI (High-Definition Multimedia Interface): Audio/video interface for transmitting uncompressed digital video and multi-channel digital audio over a single cable.

Expansion Slots: Internal sockets on the motherboard designed to accept peripheral expansion cards (such as Dedicated Graphics Cards or Network Interface Cards, e.g., PCIe slots).
